## Option 1 — Recommended (147 words; strict NHB compliance)

> Across psychology, psychiatry, and behavioural science, replicable cluster profiles and density-enriched centres are routinely interpreted as discrete human types. Yet replicability reflects statistical structure, not necessarily discrete latent kinds. Across $N = 563,886$ participants from six personality and psychopathology cohorts, we show how correlated continuous distributions generate replicable profiles, apparent density enrichment, and multi-class mixture solutions without discrete subpopulations. Analytically, we derive a sufficient spectral condition for non-collinear three-cluster quantization ($\lambda_2/\lambda_1 > 0.50764$) and an exact unimodal density-ratio identity ($|\mathbf{R}|^{-1/2}$), while showing geometrically and empirically how anisotropic correlations stabilize profile orientation. Replicating Gerlach et al.'s $K = 13$ enrichment stage on IPIP-NEO-300 ($N = 145,388$), all 13 centres converge under exact mean-shift on the nearest-neighbour KDE surface to one dominant mode with no intervening valleys, matching a parallel topographic check on IPIP-NEO-120 ($N = 410,376$). Matched continuous nulls reproduce cross-instrument Big Five and US--Czech HiTOP-SR profiles, while five linear traits outperform hard typologies across 86 life outcomes. We propose a three-audit minimum evidential standard.
